# Firgelli Full Catalog — Potentiometer Feedback + 2"/50mm Stroke Search

## Which Firgelli lines offer potentiometer feedback at all (not just Hall sensor)?

### Takeaway
Across Firgelli's own catalog pages and spec sheets, only **one** dedicated line is confirmed by the manufacturer's own spec sheet to use a potentiometer: the **Feedback Rod Linear Actuator (model prefix FA-PO)**. Every other feedback-capable line found in this search (Utility, Super Duty, Optical/Premium, Micro Pen Feedback, Industrial Heavy Duty, Bullet .50 Cal) uses Hall-effect, optical-encoder feedback, or an unspecified "Yes" feedback flag — not a potentiometer. Several non-feedback lines (Classic Rod, Heavy Duty Rod, Deluxe Rod, standard Mini) have no feedback option at all in their own spec sheets.

### Cited Findings
- Firgelli's "Feedback Rod Linear Actuator" spec sheet states: "Feedback Rod Linear Actuator is equipped with a Bourns 10 turn potentiometer allowing you to determine the position of the actuator at any point in time," with "Feedback: 10K Ohm 3-wire potentiometer, Linearity +/- 0.25%, Power Rating 1 - 1.5 W" — [Firgelli Feedback Rod spec sheet PDF](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0615/2193/files/firgelli-feedback-rod-linear-actuator-spec-sheet.pdf) (manufacturer datasheet, fetched directly and read in full).
- A Firgelli comparison table (in a blog post about feedback vs. standard actuators) lists, by product line, feedback type / force range / speed range / stroke range / IP rating: "Utility Linear Actuator: Hall Effect, 110 to 330 lbs, 0.25 to 1.0 in/s, 2 to 12 in, IP66"; "Super Duty Actuators: Hall Effect, 220 to 450 lbs, 0.3 to 0.75 in/s, 2 to 40 in, IP66"; "Optical Feedback: optical encoder, 35 to 400 lbs, 0.3 to 2.0 in/s, 1 to 30 in, IP61"; "Micro Pen (Feedback): Hall Effect, 4 to 22 lbs, 0.2 to 1.2 in/s, 1 to 4 in, IP66"; "Bullet Series 50 Cal.: feedback marked only as 'Yes' (type not specified), 500 to 1124 lbs, 0.08 to 0.48 in/s, 6 to 40 in, IP66" — [Feedback actuator vs standard actuator Guide](https://www.firgelliauto.com/blogs/news/feedback-actuator-vs-standard-actuator) (manufacturer's own blog, summarized by an automated fetch of the page; I did not see the raw table HTML myself, so treat exact numbers as reported-through-fetch, not hand-verified).
- A web search result states the Industrial Heavy Duty (2,200 lb) line "includes Hall-sensor feedback for optional position monitoring" with a 40:1 gear ratio — [search summary citing firgelliauto.com Industrial pages](https://firgelliauto.com/products/industrial-feedback-actuators) (third-party-aggregated search snippet of Firgelli's own page; not independently fetched due to repeated HTTP 429 rate-limiting on firgelliauto.com/firgelli.com during this session).
- Firgelli's own general guidance says "Firgelli only sells directional Hall sensors so you know what direction you are heading in," which is in tension with other Firgelli pages describing potentiometer and optical options — [Linear Actuator Feedback Devices: Potentiometers vs Encoders](https://www.firgelliauto.com/blogs/actuators/linear-actuator-feedback-devices-explained) (manufacturer blog, via search summary).
- The "Classic Rod Linear Actuator" spec sheet and the "Heavy Duty Rod Linear Actuator" spec sheet and the "Deluxe Rod Linear Actuator" spec sheet (all fetched/read directly, Deluxe Rod in full) contain no "Feedback" row at all — only "Dynamic Force / Static Force / Speed / Duty Cycle / Stroke / IP Rating / Input / Max Draw / Clevis End / Operational Temperature / Limit Switch / Safety Certifications / Brackets." Deluxe Rod (model FA-HF-100-12-XX): [manufacturer PDF, read in full](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0615/2193/files/firgelli-deluxe-rod-linear-actuator-spec-sheet.pdf). - The standard "Mini Linear Actuator" spec sheet (model FA-RMS-15-12-X, 15 lb dynamic force) likewise has no Feedback row — [manufacturer PDF, read in full](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0615/2193/files/Mini_Linear_Actuator_Spec_Sheet.pdf).
- A separate small actuator, the **PQ12-P** (sold by Firgelli/RobotShop but originally an Actuonix part), does have a potentiometer: "a 2 kΩ linear potentiometer with 1% linearity," read on pin 5 as a wiper voltage between the two reference pins — [PQ12 datasheet copy](https://cdn.robotshop.com/media/f/fir/rb-fir-42/pdf/actuonix-pq12-datasheet.pdf) (third-party-hosted copy of a datasheet; the part is branded/sold as Actuonix, not as a distinct "Firgelli" product line, so treat this as a caveat rather than a true Firgelli catalog line).

### Inferences
- Firgelli's potentiometer feedback option is effectively concentrated in a single low/mid-force line (FA-PO / "Feedback Rod"), while their higher-force "industrial-grade" feedback lines (Super Duty, Industrial Heavy Duty) standardized on Hall-effect sensors instead, likely because Hall sensors tolerate higher vibration/dust/higher-cycle industrial duty better than a mechanical potentiometer wiper, and don't wear out the way a resistive track does (consistent with Firgelli's own stated potentiometer limitation that "the resistive material can wear out, and the signal can become erratic" — from the Dont_Buy_a_Feedback... PDF/blog material found in search but not independently fetched in full this session).

## Of the lines with potentiometer feedback, which offer a 2-inch / 50mm stroke option specifically?

### Takeaway

The PQ12-P (Actuonix-branded, distributed by Firgelli/RobotShop) has a potentiometer but its maximum stroke is only 20mm (0.79"), far short of 2".

### Cited Findings

- A RobotShop listing independently confirms a "2\" Stroke 150 lbs Force Linear Actuator with Potentiometer Feedback" and a separate "2\" Stroke Firgelli Automation 12VDC 35lb Force Linear Actuators w/ Pot Feedback" — [RobotShop 150lb 2" listing](https://www.robotshop.com/products/2-stroke-150-lbs-force-linear-actuator-w-potentiometer); [RobotShop 35lb 2" listing](https://robotshop.com/products/2-stroke-firgelli-automation-12vdc-35lb-force-linear-actuators-w-pot-feedback) (third-party reseller listings, not manufacturer datasheets, but consistent with and corroborating the manufacturer's own FA-PO spec sheet).

## For each 2-inch potentiometer-feedback model, what is the dynamic force, speed at full load, and weight?

### Takeaway
All data below comes from Firgelli's own "Feedback Rod Linear Actuator" spec sheet PDF (fetched and read in full). The line offers three force variants, and all three share the same 2" stroke dimensional/weight spec; force and speed are inversely related within the line.

### Cited Findings — FA-PO series, 2" stroke (all figures from the manufacturer spec sheet unless noted)
- **FA-PO-35-12-XX**: Dynamic Force **35 lb (≈155.7 N)**; Static Force 70 lb (≈311.4 N); Speed (table/rated) **2.0 in/s (≈50.8 mm/s)**; Max Draw 5 A; Weight (2" stroke) **2.7 lbs (≈1.22 kg)**; Retracted/Extended length 7.9"/9.9" — [Firgelli spec sheet PDF](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0615/2193/files/firgelli-feedback-rod-linear-actuator-spec-sheet.pdf).
- **FA-PO-150-12-XX**: Dynamic Force **150 lb (≈667.2 N)**; Static Force 300 lb (≈1334.5 N); Speed (table/rated) **0.5 in/s (≈12.7 mm/s)**; Weight (2" stroke) **2.7 lbs (≈1.22 kg)** — same source.
- **FA-PO-240-12-XX**: Dynamic Force **240 lb (≈1067.5 N)**; Static Force 400 lb (≈1779.3 N); Speed (table/rated) **0.3 in/s (≈7.6 mm/s)**; Weight (2" stroke) **2.7 lbs (≈1.22 kg)** — same source. (Note: the product's marketing copy calls this line's overall ceiling "up to 200 lbs. of force," which is inconsistent with the 240 lb row in its own spec table — both figures appear verbatim on the same official sheet; flagging this as an internal inconsistency in Firgelli's own document, not something I introduced.)
- Shared specs across all three force variants: 10K Ohm 3-wire Bourns 10-turn potentiometer, linearity ±0.25%, power rating 1–1.5 W; 12 VDC input; IP54; duty cycle 20% at 100% load, 50% at 25% load, max 5 min continuous use; clevis end 1/4"; operating temp −15°F to 150°F (−26°C to 65°C) — same source.
- The spec sheet's single "Speed (inches/second)" row is the only tabulated speed value per model; it is accompanied by a "12V DC Speed vs Load" performance graph showing speed declining somewhat with increasing load for each model (plotted curves for 35/150/240 lb variants), but the graph was read only as a description of its axes and general shape (downward-sloping lines), not as precise pixel-level data points — I cannot state an exact numeric "full load" speed distinct from the tabulated value with confidence from this source.

### Inferences
- Firgelli's "Dynamic Force" rating appears to be defined as the continuous/rated force at the tabulated speed (i.e., this already represents something like "full load" performance for that model), while "Static Force" is the maximum holding/stall force with no motion. Under that reading, the tabulated "Speed (inches/second)" value is effectively the full-load speed for each model, and no-load speed would be marginally higher (per the leftmost/lowest-load end of each model's curve on the "12V DC Speed vs Load" graph), but I don't have a precise no-load number beyond "slightly above the rated value, based on the graph's shape."
- Converting to the user's target units: 35 lb ≈ 155.7 N, 150 lb ≈ 667.2 N, 240 lb ≈ 1067.5 N (using 1 lb = 4.448 N); 2 in/s = 50.8 mm/s, 0.5 in/s = 12.7 mm/s, 0.3 in/s = 7.62 mm/s (using 1 in = 25.4 mm).

### Gaps
- No manufacturer-stated distinct "no-load speed" number separate from the single tabulated "Speed (inches/second)" value — only a qualitative graph trend (speed decreases somewhat as load increases) was available from the PDF text extraction used in this session.

## Does any Firgelli model combine ≥294 N force, ≥18 mm/s at full load, 2-inch stroke, AND potentiometer feedback simultaneously?

### Takeaway
**No.** Within the only confirmed potentiometer-feedback + 2"-stroke line (FA-PO), the model with sufficient force (FA-PO-150, 667 N and FA-PO-240, 1067.5 N, both ≥294 N) falls well short of 18 mm/s (12.7 mm/s and 7.6 mm/s respectively), while the model with sufficient speed (FA-PO-35, 50.8 mm/s) falls short of 294 N (only 155.7 N). No Firgelli SKU found in this search satisfies all four criteria at once.

### Cited Findings
- See the FA-PO force/speed table above — [Firgelli Feedback Rod spec sheet PDF](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0615/2193/files/firgelli-feedback-rod-linear-actuator-spec-sheet.pdf): 35 lb/155.7N @ 50.8mm/s; 150 lb/667.2N @ 12.7mm/s; 240 lb/1067.5N @ 7.6mm/s — all at 2" stroke.

### Inferences
- Arithmetically: none of (155.7N,50.8mm/s), (667.2N,12.7mm/s), (1067.5N,7.6mm/s) satisfies "force ≥294N AND speed ≥18mm/s" simultaneously. The closest near-miss is FA-PO-150 (667N, well above 294N target) which would need roughly 1.4x more speed (12.7→18mm/s) to clear the bar.

### Gaps
- None for this specific question, given the findings above are exhaustive for the one confirmed potentiometer+2"-stroke line. (The broader gap about unverified Super Duty/Industrial pages, noted above, means I cannot rule out with 100% certainty that some other Firgelli SKU not surfaced by this search's queries combines all four criteria — but no line found in this session other than FA-PO was confirmed to have potentiometer feedback at all.)

## If no single model does, which criterion is hardest to get at the same time within Firgelli's catalog?

### Takeaway
**Speed at full load is the hardest criterion to hold onto as force increases, within the potentiometer-feedback line.** Going from the 35 lb to the 240 lb FA-PO variant, force goes up ~7x but speed drops ~6.7x (2.0 in/s → 0.3 in/s) — this is a direct trade-off, not an independent limitation. The 2-inch stroke option itself is NOT the limiting factor — it's available at all three force levels within the pot-feedback line. Losing the potentiometer option entirely (in favor of Hall-effect) is what happens if you instead look across Firgelli's catalog for a line built to deliver both high force (Super Duty: 220–450 lb) and reasonable speed (Super Duty: up to 0.75 in/s ≈ 19mm/s) at a 2" stroke — Super Duty would actually satisfy the 294N/18mm/s/2"-stroke numeric targets, but only with Hall-effect feedback, not potentiometer.

### Cited Findings
- FA-PO line speed vs. force (manufacturer spec sheet, cited above): 2.0 in/s @ 35 lb → 0.5 in/s @ 150 lb → 0.3 in/s @ 240 lb. All three keep the 2" stroke option and the potentiometer feedback; only speed collapses as force rises.
- Super Duty Actuators are reported (via the Firgelli comparison-table search summary) as 220–450 lb force, 0.3–0.75 in/s speed, 2–40 in stroke, Hall Effect feedback, IP66 — [Feedback actuator vs standard actuator Guide](https://www.firgelliauto.com/blogs/news/feedback-actuator-vs-standard-actuator) (search-summarized, not independently fetched this session — see Gap above). If accurate, a 220 lb (≈979N) Super Duty unit near its top speed end (≈0.75in/s ≈19mm/s, though that top speed likely corresponds to its lowest-force 65lb variant rather than the 220lb one specifically — the summary did not break out speed-per-force-tier) could plausibly hit ≥294N and ≥18mm/s at a 2" stroke, but with Hall-effect feedback, not a potentiometer.

### Inferences
- Within Firgelli's catalog, there appears to be a structural choice the company made: the potentiometer-feedback product (FA-PO) is built on a lower-power motor/gear train (1–1.5W power rating, max 5A draw) that trades maximum force for stroke-range flexibility, while its higher-force/higher-speed feedback products (Super Duty, Industrial) were engineered around Hall-effect sensors instead — likely so the feedback sensor could handle the higher duty/vibration without the wear/noise problems Firgelli's own materials associate with potentiometers (wiper wear, electrical noise, limited turn range inside a gear train). So the real hardest trade-off is: **wanting potentiometer feedback specifically (not just "feedback") locks you into the FA-PO power/force envelope, and within that envelope, more force always costs you roughly proportional speed.**

### Gaps
- This inference about engineering rationale (why Firgelli chose Hall effect for higher-force lines) is my own reasoning from the pattern in the data, not a stated manufacturer explanation — flagged as inference, not fact.
- Could not confirm, from a manufacturer page I read directly, whether Super Duty's speed figures break down per force tier (e.g., is 0.75 in/s only available on the weakest 65 lb variant, or also on the 220 lb variant?) — this would need a direct, non-rate-limited fetch of the Super Duty product page or its per-stroke spec PDF to resolve.
